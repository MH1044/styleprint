# The Camping Trip That Cured Me of Camping Trips

Three of us went up to the lake in October, which should have been our first warning, because October up there means one thing is certain and it isn't the weather. We'd planned it in July, over beers, the way all terrible trips get planned — with maximum enthusiasm and zero research.

The drive alone took five hours longer than the map said, on account of a detour that wasn't on the map at all, just a hand-painted sign nailed to a tree. We followed it anyway. Why wouldn't we. Nothing bad has ever come from following a hand-painted sign into the woods.

We got to the campsite with maybe forty minutes of daylight left, which is exactly enough time to realize that nobody had actually pitched this particular tent before and the instructions were in a language none of us could identify, possibly Finnish. Dave insisted he "basically knew" how it worked. Dave did not basically know how it worked. We slept that night under a tent that was, structurally, more of a large tarp with ambitions.

It rained. Of course it rained. It rained the specific way that only happens on trips you were excited about, a cold, committed rain that found every gap in the tarp-tent and introduced itself personally to my sleeping bag. I woke up at 3 a.m. in what I can only describe as a puddle with opinions, and I just lay there doing the math on how many hours until sunrise. The answer was four. Four hours is a very long time to lie in a puddle.

Here's the part I actually love telling people: around 4 a.m., soaked, furious, we gave up trying to sleep and just built a fire instead, which took forty minutes of wet matches and Dave's genuine engineering background finally paying off for the first time all trip. We sat around that fire until the sun came up, barely talking, just watching steam come off our socks, and it was — and I did not expect to feel this — one of the best mornings I've had in years.

That's the annoying truth about trips like this. The parts that go right don't make the story. Nobody wants to hear about the sandwich that was fine. They want to hear about the puddle, the Finnish tent, Dave's confidence collapsing in real time. And somewhere between the collapse and the fire, something actually good happens that you couldn't have planned for, because planning isn't really the point of a trip like this.

Would I do it again? Not in October. Not with that tent, which we left there, honestly, folded badly into a trash bag and shoved in the trunk like evidence. But yes, basically, I'd do it again. Just with a better tarp and lower expectations, which might be the same piece of advice twice.

We still talk about that trip more than any of the ones that went smoothly. Funny how that works.
